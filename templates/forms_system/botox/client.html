What is being injected?

Botulinum Toxin (which may be known by brand names such as Azzalure, Botox, Evolus, or Bocouture) is a purified protein derived from the bacterium Clostridium botulinum. It works by temporarily relaxing targeted muscles or reducing sweating by blocking nerve signals. The effects usually last between 2 to 6 months, although this can vary from person to person.

In many cases, Botulinum Toxin is used 'off-label', meaning it is safely and commonly used in areas not specifically listed in the product's official licence. This is standard practice and based on clinical experience and best outcomes.

What are the possible side effects and risks?

While Botulinum Toxin treatments are generally safe, some people may experience temporary side effects, which usually settle within a few days. These may include:

  • Mild headache
  • Swelling, bruising, or redness at the injection site
  • Small amount of bleeding or discomfort during treatment
  • Twitching, itching, or puffiness around the eyes
  • Temporary numbness or altered sensation in the treated area

Less common side effects include:

  • Allergic reactions, including very rare but serious reactions like anaphylaxis
  • Asymmetry, where one side of the face may appear slightly different from the other
  • Temporary drooping of facial features (such as the eyelid or brow)
  • Dry eyes, or more rarely, blurred or double vision

Every individual responds differently to treatment, and although very rare, there may be unknown risks specific to your body or medical history.
