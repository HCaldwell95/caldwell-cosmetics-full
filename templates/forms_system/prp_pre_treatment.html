PRP — Pre-Treatment Advice

Please read and follow this advice before your appointment to help achieve the best possible result and reduce the risk of complications.

  1. Stay well hydrated in the days leading up to your appointment. Drink plenty of water — good hydration makes the blood draw easier and supports the quality of your plasma.
  2. Eat a light meal before your appointment. Arriving on an empty stomach is not recommended as it can increase the risk of feeling faint during the blood draw.
  3. Arrive with clean skin. The treatment area should be free from makeup, moisturiser, perfume, and any other skincare products.
  4. Avoid blood-thinning medications such as aspirin, ibuprofen (Nurofen/Motrin), naproxen, and Vitamin E supplements for at least 48 hours before your appointment, unless they are prescribed by your GP. Do not stop any prescribed medication without consulting your doctor first.
  5. Avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours before treatment, as it can thin the blood and reduce the effectiveness of the PRP.
  6. Avoid retinoids and strong active skincare (e.g. retinol, glycolic acid, AHAs/BHAs) for at least 5–7 days before treatment on the area to be treated.
  7. Avoid recent cosmetic treatments such as chemical peels, laser, microneedling, or dermal fillers for at least 2 weeks prior to PRP, unless specifically advised otherwise by your practitioner.
  8. Avoid sun exposure and ensure any tan (real or fake) has fully faded before treatment. Do not use sunbeds or prolonged sun exposure in the month prior to treatment.
  9. Inform your practitioner of any recent vaccinations (within 2 weeks), recent illness, or any changes to your health or medications since completing your consultation form.
  10. Avoid smoking for at least 24 hours before treatment, as nicotine can impair the body's healing response.

Important Reminders

  • If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive, please notify your practitioner — PRP is not suitable during this time.
  • If you have a blood disorder, are taking anticoagulants, or have been diagnosed with cancer (especially blood cancer), please discuss this with your practitioner before proceeding.
  • Wear comfortable, loose-fitting clothing to allow easy access to your arm for the blood draw.
