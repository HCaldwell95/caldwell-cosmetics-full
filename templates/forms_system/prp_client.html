About PRP Treatment

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy uses your own blood to promote natural skin rejuvenation and healing. A small amount of blood is drawn from your arm and placed in a centrifuge, which separates the platelet-rich plasma from the red blood cells. The concentrated plasma — rich in growth factors — is then injected or applied to the treatment area to stimulate collagen production, improve skin texture, and support tissue repair.

PRP is commonly used for skin rejuvenation, hair restoration, and under-eye treatment. Results are gradual and typically become visible from 4–6 weeks after treatment, with continued improvement over 3–6 months. Multiple sessions are usually recommended for optimal results.

Because PRP uses your own blood, the risk of an allergic reaction is very low. However, as with any injectable treatment, there are potential side effects and risks outlined below.

Possible side effects and risks

  • Bruising, swelling, and redness at the treatment site — common and expected, usually resolving within 3–5 days
  • Mild discomfort during the blood draw and at injection sites
  • Temporary skin sensitivity or tenderness
  • Risk of infection (rare — sterile technique is used throughout)
  • Temporary worsening of the skin's appearance before improvement is seen
  • No guarantee of results — individual response to PRP varies
  • In rare cases, little or no visible improvement
